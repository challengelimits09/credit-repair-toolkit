Your 7-step game plan

  1. Pull all three reports (free, weekly). Get Equifax, Experian & TransUnion from AnnualCreditReport.com — the only federally authorized free source. Reports are now free every week. (See Resources.)
  2. Lock things down. Freeze your credit at all three bureaus and opt out of prescreened offers — stops new fraud and junk inquiries while you work.
  3. Enter your info & list every negative item. Go to My Info, then Negative Items. Add collections, charge-offs, late payments, wrong personal info, and unauthorized inquiries.
  4. Get your strategy. The Strategy tab tells you exactly which letters to send for each item and in what order.
  5. Generate & mail the letters. The Letters tab fills out 16 different professional letters with your details. Always send by certified mail with return receipt.
  6. Track the clock. Bureaus get 30 days to investigate. Log each letter in the Tracker; it calculates deadlines and tells you the next move.
  7. Escalate. No response or a bogus "verified"? Send a Method-of-Verification demand, dispute with the furnisher directly, and file a CFPB complaint.

✅ What you CAN legally remove

  • Information that is inaccurate (wrong balance, wrong dates, "late" when you paid on time)
  • Information that is incomplete or unverifiable — if the furnisher can't prove it, it must be deleted
  • Accounts that aren't yours or resulted from identity theft
  • Obsolete items past the 7-year (or 10-year for Ch. 7 bankruptcy) limit
  • Unauthorized hard inquiries you never approved
  • Accurate late payments — via a goodwill request (the creditor's discretion)

⛔ What this toolkit will NOT do (it's illegal)

  • Disputing accurate information with false statements — that's fraud.
  • CPNs / "credit privacy numbers" as SSN substitutes — federal identity fraud.
  • Creating a "new credit identity" or "file segregation."
  • Promising a guaranteed score jump — no one can.

Only dispute items you genuinely believe are inaccurate, incomplete, outdated, or unverifiable. That's both the legal path and the effective one.

Know Your Rights

The laws that make all of this work — in plain English. Citing them correctly is what gets results.

The two laws on your side

FCRA — Fair Credit Reporting Act (15 U.S.C. §1681). Governs the credit bureaus and the companies that report data ("furnishers"). It gives you the right to accurate reporting, to dispute errors, and to have unverifiable items deleted.

FDCPA — Fair Debt Collection Practices Act (15 U.S.C. §1692). Governs third-party debt collectors. It lets you demand validation, stop harassment, and control how they contact you.

Key FCRA sections you'll cite
  • §609 (1681g) — Your right to disclosure: get everything in your file and the sources of it. Useful to force out documentation behind an item.
  • §611 (1681i) — The dispute engine. Bureaus must reinvestigate within 30 days (45 if you add info mid-stream). Unverifiable info must be deleted. §611(a)(7) gives you the right to the method of verification.
  • §623 (1681s-2) — Furnisher duties. You can dispute directly with the creditor; they can't keep reporting data they know is wrong.
  • §605 (1681c) — Time limits: most negatives drop off after 7 years; Chapter 7 bankruptcy after 10.
  • §605B (1681c-2) — Identity-theft block: with an FTC report, bureaus must block fraudulent items within 4 business days.
Key FDCPA tools
  • §1692g — Validation. Within 30 days of a collector's first contact, demand proof. They must stop collecting until they validate.
  • §1692c(c) — Cease communication. You can order a collector to stop contacting you (use with care — it can prompt a lawsuit on a valid debt).
  • §1692k — Damages. Violations can entitle you to up to $1,000 plus attorney's fees. Save proof of every violation.
How long do negative items last?
ItemReporting limit (FCRA §605)
Late payments7 years from the date of the missed payment
Collections & charge-offs7 years from ~180 days after the original delinquency (≈7½ yrs total)
Chapter 13 bankruptcy7 years from filing
Chapter 7 bankruptcy10 years from filing
Civil judgments / lawsuits7 years (or longer if your state's SOL is longer)
Hard inquiries2 years (and only affect your score for ~1)
Closed accounts in good standingUp to 10 years (these help you — leave them)
Don't confuse the credit-reporting clock (FCRA) with the statute of limitations to sue (state law). They're different lengths. And beware: making a payment or even promising to pay an old debt can restart the SOL in many states.
Medical debt — the 2026 reality

The CFPB's 2025 rule that would have banned medical debt from credit reports was struck down by a federal court in July 2025 and is not in force. However, the three bureaus' voluntary changes still stand: paid medical collections are removed, medical collections under $500 are removed, and there's a 12-month grace period before any medical collection can appear. If a medical item violates these, dispute it.

Dispute reasons that actually work (cheat sheet)
  • "This is not my account / I never opened this."
  • "This account was never late — the payment history is wrong."
  • "The balance / amount reported is incorrect."
  • "This was paid/settled but shows a balance owing."
  • "The dates (opened, last activity, first delinquency) are wrong" — wrong DOFD can keep an item on illegally past 7 years.
  • "This is a duplicate of another tradeline."
  • "This account is past the 7-year reporting limit."
  • "I never authorized this inquiry."
  • "You reported this as 'verified' — prove it: send me the method of verification."
Smart habits that build score while you dispute
  • Keep credit-card utilization under ~30% (under 10% is ideal). This is ~30% of your score and moves fast.
  • Never miss a current payment — payment history is the biggest factor.
  • Don't close old cards; age of history helps you.
  • Ask an on-time family member to add you as an authorized user on an old, low-balance card.
  • Avoid new hard inquiries while repairing.
  • Consider a secured card or credit-builder loan to add positive history.

Resources & Addresses

Verified contacts. Mailing addresses for disputes differ from corporate HQ — these go to the right department.

Free credit reports

AnnualCreditReport.com — the only federally authorized source. All three bureaus, now free every week. Avoid look-alike sites.

Specialty reports (also free): LexisNexis, ChexSystems (banking), and the work-history report from The Work Number — worth checking for errors too.

Stop new damage

Opt out of prescreened offers: optoutprescreen.com or 1-888-567-8688.

Freeze your credit (free) at each bureau — see cards above. A freeze blocks new accounts; lift it temporarily when you apply for credit.

File a complaint (free leverage)

CFPB: consumerfinance.gov/complaint · (855) 411-2372. Companies almost always respond within 15 days.

FTC: reportfraud.ftc.gov.

Your State Attorney General — search "[your state] attorney general consumer complaint".

Identity theft

IdentityTheft.gov — file the official FTC Identity Theft Report you'll need for a §605B block, and get a recovery plan.

Free / low-cost human help

  • NACA (consumer attorneys) — consumeradvocates.org. Many FCRA/FDCPA lawyers work free to you (the law makes violators pay fees).
  • Nonprofit credit counseling — NFCC.org.
  • Legal aid — LawHelp.org for free help if you qualify.
